## My Capstone Project

My capstone project investigated whether Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy could enable rapid differentiation between antibiotic-resistant and non-resistant Escherichia coli strains. Antibiotic susceptibility was assessed using the Kirby–Bauer disk diffusion method, while FTIR spectroscopy was used to measure biochemical patterns associated with cellular components such as proteins, lipids and carbohydrates. The project examined whether differences in these spectral patterns could complement conventional susceptibility-testing results.

## The Bioinformatics Component

The bioinformatics component used R and RStudio to organize and visualize FTIR spectral data from three experimental replicates. The datasets were stored as TSV files and imported into R, where they were combined into a single graph. This approach made it easier to compare the replicates, evaluate repeatability and identify areas of consistency or variation among the spectral patterns.
The combined visualization provided a clearer representation of the experimental data than reviewing three separate graphs. It allowed the replicate results to be examined together and helped determine whether the observed FTIR patterns were consistent across repeated trials. The resulting graph also provided a concise visual output that could be used in the project report and scientific poster.

## Why It Strengthened My Capstone

Using R strengthened the capstone by providing an organized and reproducible method for comparing multiple datasets. It connected laboratory-generated FTIR results to computational data analysis, making the findings easier to interpret and communicate. This component also demonstrated how bioinformatics and visualization tools can support quality assessment and improve the presentation of experimental research.
